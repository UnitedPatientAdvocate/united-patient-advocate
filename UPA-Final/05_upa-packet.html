Download Letters 2 and 3 from this packet. Send Letter 2 (line-item concern review) to Your Provider with your highlighted itemized bill attached. Send Letter 3 (lab rate inquiry) to both Your Provider and Outside Service Provider. You can send both at the same time.

Wait for Their Response
14–45 days

The billing department may review your letters and respond in writing. They may request additional information, issue a corrected statement, or decline the review request. If you receive a corrected bill showing the billing line corrected, your request may be resolved. If they decline, see Step 5.

5
If No Response or Decline — Escalate
Only if needed · Varies

If the billing department does not respond within 45 days or declines without a clear explanation, you can ask for the hospital's Patient Financial Advocate, patient representative, or billing supervisor, or file a complaint with your state's insurance commissioner. Many disputes are handled before this step is needed.

Mistakes To Avoid — Page 7 of 11

Protect Your Position
The Most Common Mistakes
— and How to Avoid Them

None of these are your fault if they've already happened. But knowing them now means you stay in the strongest position possible going forward.

⚠️
Paying the full bill before requesting itemization
Once you pay, recovering money can be significantly harder. Request the itemized bill first, and ask the provider in writing to pause collection activity on the reviewed amount while the request is being processed.
⚠️
Making partial payments on disputed amounts
Paying any portion of a charge under review can sometimes be read as acceptance of the full bill. If you need to make a payment, only pay the confirmed portions and state in writing that the rest is under review.
⚠️
Calling without keeping written records
Every phone call should be followed by a note: who you spoke with, what they said, and the date. Write down any reference number they give you. Hospitals can lose records. Your notes protect you if things escalate.
⚠️
Accepting "that's how our billing works" as a final answer
A billing system explanation is still worth reviewing. A line-item concern can be reviewed regardless of how it was generated. If you hit a wall, calmly ask to speak with a supervisor or the Patient Advocate office.
⚠️
Going silent during the review process
Keep communicating. Silence can be treated as abandonment of your request. If you haven't received a response after 30 days, follow up in writing. Active reviews are protected — quiet ones are not.
⚠️
Getting frustrated on the phone
The billing representative you speak with almost certainly did not create the issue. Being calm and kind gets better results. You are more likely to be helped quickly when the call feels cooperative rather than confrontational.
💚
You are doing the right thing. The fact that you are reading this page means you are ahead of most patients. Most people pay without ever asking a question. You asked — and that alone puts you in a stronger position.

Your Patient Rights — Page 8 of 11
⚖️
Simple. Empowering. Yours.
These Are Common
Billing Protections and Request Paths.

You do not need a lawyer to use these request paths. You just need to ask — in writing, calmly, with a copy kept for yourself. That is exactly what this packet helps you do.

📄
Itemized Bill Request
Request a Fully Itemized Bill
You can request a complete, line-by-line itemized statement from the provider. Many providers can provide it on request; timing and requirements vary by state, plan, provider, and dispute process.
📋
CMS Claims Processing Manual · Chapter 1, §80.7
Right to Request Correction of Line-Item Reviews
CMS billing guidance can support questions about duplicate billing lines. If a billing line item appears twice on a single visit, you have grounds to request a written explanation and correction if the provider confirms a duplicate entry. billing line item appears twice on your bill on the relevant itemized bill lines.
🏥
No Surprises Act · Public Law 116-260 (2022)
Review In-Network Rate Protections for Ancillary Services
If you received care at an in-network facility, No Surprises Act protections may limit out-of-network billing for certain services ordered by in-network providers — including some lab work — without required notice and consent. Your Outside Service Provider lab was ordered during your in-network visit at Your Provider.
⏸️
Fair Debt Collection Practices Act
Request a Collection Pause While Your Request Is Active
Once you submit a written billing review request, ask the provider or collector to pause collection activity on the reviewed amount while the review is active. Our prepared letters include this request. Credit reporting treatment varies by account, timing, collector status, and dispute type.
👤
Common Hospital Support Channel
Ask to Speak with a Patient Financial Advocate
Many hospitals have a Patient Financial Advocate, Patient Representative, or billing supervisor who can help route billing concerns directly. If your letters don't get results, simply call the hospital and ask: "I'd like to speak with the Patient Financial Advocate, please."
