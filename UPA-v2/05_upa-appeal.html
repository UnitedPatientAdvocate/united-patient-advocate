The most common is medical necessity — representing over 60% of all denials — and it has the strongest grounds for appeal.
No. The ACA gives every patient the right to an internal appeal and an independent external review — no attorney required. A well-documented appeal citing the insurer's own clinical criteria is often more effective than a legal brief. We give you exactly that documentation.
Insurers are required by the ACA to respond to internal appeals within 30 days for non-urgent claims, and 72 hours for urgent/expedited appeals. If your internal appeal is denied, you can request an independent external review — those decisions are typically issued within 45 days and are legally binding on the insurer.
Grandfathered plans may be exempt from some ACA appeal requirements, and self-funded employer plans follow ERISA appeal rules rather than ACA. Our system detects your plan type from the denial letter and applies the correct legal framework. ERISA appeals have different deadlines (60–180 days depending on plan documents) — we cover both.
