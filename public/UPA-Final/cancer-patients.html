What We Find

Seven Places Cancer Bills
Quietly Overcharge You

Oncology billing codes are dense, layered, and frequently miscoded — even by experienced billing departments. These are the errors we find most often.

Chemotherapy Drug Dosage Billing Errors
Chemo drugs are billed by dose weight (mg). When your actual dose is less than the billed amount — which is extremely common — you're charged for drugs you never received. Each infusion session is a separate exposure.
Extremely Common
Duplicate Infusion Administration Charges
Hospitals routinely bill the infusion administration fee once per drug administered, even when drugs are given sequentially in the same session. Rules require fees to be bundled — not stacked.
High Frequency
Out-of-Network Anesthesia on In-Network Surgeries
You chose an in-network hospital and surgeon — but the anesthesiologist was independently contracted and out-of-network. This surprise billing is now federally restricted under the No Surprises Act, yet hospitals still try to collect.
Federally Disputable
Clinical Trial Billing Violations
Federal law prohibits hospitals from billing patients (or their insurance) for the experimental treatment, device, or drug in a clinical trial. Only routine care costs are billable. Violations are serious and 100% disputable.
Federal Violation if Billed
Radiation Treatment Code Errors
Radiation therapy uses highly specific CPT codes based on technique, field complexity, and device. Simple vs. complex field codes carry dramatically different reimbursement rates — and miscoding consistently goes in the hospital's favor.
Code-Level Audit Needed
Post-Surgery Follow-Up Bundling Violations
Follow-up visits within the global surgical period are supposed to be bundled into the original surgery fee — not billed separately. Cancer surgeries have 90-day global periods. Separate billing during this window is a violation.
90-Day Window Applies
Denials for "Experimental" When Standard of Care
Insurers routinely deny proven oncology treatments — immunotherapies, targeted therapies, certain surgeries — by labeling them "experimental" or "not medically necessary." These denials are appealed and overturned more than any other denial category.
Most Commonly Overturned
Why oncology bills are different from everything else

A routine hospitalization might have 20–30 billing codes. A cancer treatment course — surgery, chemo, radiation, imaging, labs, infusions, follow-ups — can generate hundreds of line items across dozens of visits. Each one is a separate opportunity for error. Oncology billing departments are among the most complex in healthcare, and errors are not exceptions. They are routine.
